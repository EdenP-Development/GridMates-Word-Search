## Editorial points established by sources

- NASA identifies **Charon** as Pluto's moon, **Rhea, Dione, Mimas, Iapetus, Hyperion** as Saturnian moons, and **Proteus** as a moon of Neptune. Valid but specialist names; context avoids confusion with mythology or personal names. [Charon](https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/pluto/moons/charon/), [Saturn moons](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/moons/), [Proteus](https://science.nasa.gov/neptune/moons/proteus/).
- NASA documents **axial tilt, tidal locking, polar ice caps and planetary rotation**. These are meaningful physical features, not filler, although some are better in *Planetary Science* than a cozy general *Our Solar System* chapter. [NASA tidal locking](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/tidal-locking/), [Neptune tilt](https://science.nasa.gov/neptune/neptune-facts/), [planetary ice caps](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/frozen-ice-on-earth-and-well-beyond/). **Albedo** is a real science term for reflected light; keep advanced. [NASA albedo](https://mynasadata.larc.nasa.gov/mini-lessonactivity/what-albedo).
- NC State Extension verifies **Coreopsis, Delphinium, Columbine, Dianthus, Astilbe, Helenium, Lupine, Yarrow, Shasta Daisy, Penstemon, Monarda and Anemone** as actual flowering plant names. Keep their *flower-border context* while exploring familiar names such as Tickseed, Larkspur, Carnation, Bee Balm and Windflower. A botanical genus is not automatically equivalent to one common-name species, and familiar names need separate review. [NC State Gardener Plant Toolbox](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/).
- National Park Service uses **animal tracks**, **wildlife signs**, and **echolocation** in real outdoor education; USDA/Forest Service recognizes **sweetgrass** habitats; Cambridge documents **thistledown**. These are usable review terms with distinct reading levels. [NPS tracks](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/animaltracks.htm), [NPS signs](https://www.nps.gov/planyourvisit/event-details.htm?id=1326C199-A461-81CA-B48D613F1C583077), [NPS echolocation](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/bats/echolocation.htm), [Forest Service sweetgrass](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/hieodo), [Cambridge thistledown](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/thistledown).
- **RIVERSTONE** remains a *word-form issue*. River stones/river rock are known, but evidence so far is insufficient to require one-word `Riverstone` instead of a visually natural *River Stone*. Preserve original source and HOLD for display and familiarity review. [River stones contextual example](https://www.idea.org/words/river_stones).
